A backtest where only the way of buying changes. From the 2009-06-25 listing, at every close, one side fixes the amount at $1, the other fixes the quantity at 1 share. UPRO closed just above $1 on day one, so both start with almost the same money. The amounts differ, so the axis and bars are multiples of money put in.
Fixing the amount buys more shares when the price is low. Average cost (money in ÷ shares bought) came to $7.37 for $1 a day and $30.24 for 1 share a day. Fixing the amount pulls the cost toward the harmonic mean of the closes, fixing the quantity toward the arithmetic mean — and the harmonic mean is always smaller. The ticker sets how wide the gap gets, not who wins. On the Nasdaq 100 it was 3.5x.
